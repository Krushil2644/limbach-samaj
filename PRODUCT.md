## Users

Three audiences arrive on the same page with different jobs:

- **Limbach families already in the community**, mostly across the Greater Toronto Area. Usually on a phone, in the evening, checking whether there's an event worth bringing their parents to and how to register.
- **Newcomers and second-generation members** who feel loosely connected to the Samaj and are deciding whether this is *their* community. They need to see people, not policies.
- **Prospective sponsors and advertisers**, on a laptop, deciding whether this is a real, well-run organisation worth $101–$151 of visibility.

All three are served by the same thing: visible evidence of an active, dignified community.

## Product Purpose

The website is the Samaj's public face between gatherings. It exists so that someone who has never attended can understand what this community is and feel drawn to it, and so that someone who already belongs can find the next event and register without friction.

Success is not clicks. Success is a visitor coming away with the impression that this is a serious, warm, long-standing organisation — and knowing when the next gathering is.

## Brand Personality

**Dignified, warm, credible.**

The Samaj is a cultural and devotional community, not an events business. The voice is welcoming without being casual, proud without being loud. It speaks the way a respected elder introduces the family to a guest: unhurried, specific, generous.

Emotional goal: *belonging*. A visitor should feel this is a place with history and care behind it.

The motto is the spine of the brand: **Aharnish Sevamahe** — eternally in service of mankind at every moment.

## Anti-references

- **Festival-clipart Indian community sites.** Marigold gradients, mandala border ornaments, diya clip art, rainbow saturation. The first-order reflex for this category and the single easiest way to look amateur.
- **Editorial-magazine affectation.** Display serif italics, drop caps, mono metadata labels, ruled three-column grids. The second-order reflex — avoiding festive by landing in the other saturated lane.
- **SaaS landing-page grammar.** Uppercase tracked eyebrows above every section, identical icon-card triplets, hero metric rows, gradient-clipped headline text.
- **Event-ticketing energy.** Countdown urgency, "Book now!" pressure. This is a community, not a funnel.

## Design Principles

1. **Photographs carry the feeling; type carries the authority.** The emotional case for this community is made by real images of real gatherings. Words should be specific and calm, never do the emotional work photography should be doing.
2. **Show the community, don't describe it.** "Strong Community / Regular Events / Community Support" as three icon cards asserts what a single photograph of 200 people at Garba proves.
3. **Be concrete.** Real dates, real venues, real prices, real names. Specificity is what makes an organisation feel real to a newcomer and trustworthy to a sponsor.
4. **Dignity over decoration.** Devotional imagery and Sanskrit are treated with restraint — given space, never gradient-filled, animated, or used as texture.
5. **One accent, used decisively.** The terracotta is the Samaj's colour. Everything else is structure.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **WCAG 2.1 AA.** Body text ≥4.5:1, large text ≥3:1, verified in both light and dark themes.
- **Bilingual typography.** Devanagari must render correctly and legibly; the display family is chosen partly because it carries Devanagari.
- **Reduced motion is a first-class path**, not an afterthought. Every reveal has a `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative.
- **Motion must never gate content.** Pages are prerendered to static HTML; any animation that starts from `opacity: 0` and relies on JS would ship the page blank to crawlers and to anyone whose JS fails. Content is visible by default; motion enhances.
- **Wide age range.** Members include seniors. Tap targets ≥44px, no critical information conveyed by colour alone, generous body sizing.
